New maternity wing opens at Riverton General

The £21 million building has 28 private rooms and a dedicated birthing pool suite.

By Tom Ashworth · Riverton, 16 Nov · 2026-11-16

A new maternity wing opened at Riverton General Hospital on Monday, giving families private rooms for the first time and ending years of crowded shared wards.

The £21 million building has 28 single rooms, each with space for a partner to stay overnight, and four birthing suites, two of them with pools. A bright atrium and a garden courtyard connect the wing to the older hospital buildings.

Why it matters

The old unit, built in 1968, delivered about 3,400 babies a year in a space designed for 2,000. Midwives said that during busy periods women in labour were sometimes moved between corridors for lack of a free room. Head of midwifery Sarah Whitcombe said the difference was already clear in the first weekend.

Women tell us they feel calmer when they have a door they can close. That is not a luxury, it changes how labour goes.

The wing was paid for mostly by central government, with £2.4 million raised locally through a campaign that included sponsored walks, school collections and a charity dinner at the Assembly Rooms.

The old maternity unit will close in January and be converted into outpatient clinics, easing pressure on the main entrance. Parents-to-be can book tours of the new wing on the hospital website.

Staffing remains a worry. The trust says it has filled 22 of 26 new posts and is recruiting midwives from other regions. Unions have warned that the building alone will not fix shortages.
