Give the new bus lanes a fair chance

Three weeks of grumbling is not evidence. Let the town see a full year of data.

By Margaret Doyle, Editor · Riverton · 2026-11-12

It took less than a week for the letters to start arriving. The new bus lanes on Station Road, painted red in October, have been called a folly, a disaster and, in one memorable message, an act of war on the motorist.

Let us calm down. A traffic scheme is not a verdict on anyone's character, and three weeks is far too short a time to know whether it works. Any change to a road makes the first month worse. Drivers do not yet know where to queue, buses have not yet learned the new timings and the shops have not yet worked out where deliveries can stop.

What the numbers say so far

The council's own counts show that the number 4 bus now takes eleven minutes to cover a stretch that used to take seventeen at the evening peak. Car journeys along the same road are about two minutes slower. That is a trade. Whether it is a good trade depends on how many people sit on the bus and how many in the car, and we do not yet have a year of figures to say.

There are real complaints. Residents of Ferry Lane say the diversion has pushed cars past their front doors. Blue badge holders are right to ask where the new drop-off points are. The council should answer these questions plainly and quickly, and it has been too slow to do so.

The test

But the answer to a flawed detail is to fix the detail, not to rip out the scheme. If buses are faster and more reliable, more people will use them, and fewer cars will clog the town centre for everyone, including those who still drive.

Councillors should commit now to a review in twelve months, with the data published in full, and to scrapping the lanes if they fail. In the meantime, readers who dislike them might try the number 4. They may be surprised.

A road is judged by how many people it moves, not how many cars.
