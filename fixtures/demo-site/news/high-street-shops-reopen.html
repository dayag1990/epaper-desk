Empty units on Bridge Street fill up as rents fall

Seven shops have signed leases since the summer, the highest figure since 2019.

By Olivia Marsh · Riverton, 10 Nov · 2026-11-10

Seven empty units on Bridge Street have been let since the summer, the strongest run of lettings in the town centre since before the pandemic, according to figures from the Riverton Business Improvement District.

The newcomers include a bookbinder, a repair café, a plant shop and a small bakery. Landlords have cut asking rents by an average of 18 per cent and many are offering three months rent-free to tenants who sign multi-year leases.

A different kind of high street

BID manager Colin Haywood said the new businesses were smaller and more specialised than the chains that left. Most employ two to five people and open six days a week. He cautioned that the vacancy rate, at 11 per cent, remained above the national average.

Shop owner Amara Okafor opened a plant and pottery store in September after losing her office job. She said low rent was what made the risk affordable.

A year ago I could not have afforded this street. Now the sums work, if I am careful.

The council is considering a business-rates discount for independent traders in its next budget. A decision is expected in February.

Footfall counters on Bridge Street recorded a 6 per cent rise in October compared with the same month last year, the third monthly rise in a row.
